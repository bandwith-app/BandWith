Calendar and gigs

One calendar holds every gig, rehearsal and personal date. You create gigs from here, and run each one from its own workspace: lineup, fees, setlist, rider.

The calendar

The calendar

Tap Calendar in the bottom bar. From top to bottom: the filters, open rehearsal polls, the month grid with its legend, and the Upcoming list.

  • Type filter: ALL, GIGS, REHEARSALS, QUOTES, DRAFTS; the selected one shows how many events it holds (filters).
  • Band filter, above it, only with two or more bands: All bands, one pill per band, Personal.
  • Rehearsal scheduling ({count}): polls open for votes and proposals waiting for an admin (rehearsal polls).
  • Grid: Previous month / Next month (the arrows at the top left) or a sideways swipe; Today at the top right when you are on another month. Each day takes the colour of its event, a small number means several events, and today has a ring.
  • Legend: gig, rehearsal, quote (admins only), personal; admins with drafts also see draft · only you (a dashed, faded square).
  • Upcoming: one card per event, soonest first; recurring rehearsals get a card for each of their next dates.
  • At the bottom, {count} past events with the Past events archive link; See all next to Upcoming opens it too.

The event card

  • Colour and type (GIG, REHEARSAL, QUOTE, DRAFT, PERSONAL) and a status chip such as CONFIRMED, WEEKLY, SENT or DRAFT · ONLY YOU.
  • The band name (if you are in several bands); date, start time, venue and address.
  • Two meters: on the left LINEUP (gigs and quotes) or GOING (rehearsals); on the right FEE (admins), YOUR FEE (members), QUOTE (quotes) or LENGTH (rehearsals).
  • A warning such as 2 details missing when the venue, date or fee is missing: it opens the Missing info list, then Edit opens the event. Admins also see No end time, No client info and No team assigned.
  • Sync with a turning arrow: an event saved offline that has not reached the server yet (offline).
Every event of the bands they manage, except gigs they declined themselves; quotes, drafts, the QUOTES and DRAFTS filters, band fees on the cards and the create buttons.
Only the gigs and rehearsals an admin added them to and they have not declined, plus their own personal gigs. Never the admins’ quotes or drafts; cards show YOUR FEE, never the band fee.
Not on the band calendar: their gigs are in Deputy gigs (what a deputy sees).

With nothing scheduled, the list shows Nothing scheduled, with Gig, Rehearsal and Quote for admins and Propose rehearsal for members. With a filter on, Show All Events clears it.

Filter the calendar by type or band

  1. On Calendar, tap a type pill: ALL, GIGS, REHEARSALS, QUOTES or DRAFTS. The grid, the legend and the list follow it.
  2. With two or more bands, tap a pill in the row above: All bands, a band or Personal.
  3. ALL and All bands show everything again.

The filters combine (GIGS + one band = that band’s gigs only). The band filter only narrows: it never shows you more than you can already see.

QUOTES and DRAFTS appear for the bands they manage. Picking a band where they are only a member hides those filters, the fees and the create buttons, as for any member.
Only ALL, GIGS and REHEARSALS.
  • Personal appears only when you have personal gigs; it has no band fees and no create buttons (personal gigs are added from Create).
  • If you leave a band, or are down to one, a band filter that no longer exists goes back to All bands on its own.
  • With one band selected, a new gig lands in that band.

Search events

  1. On Calendar, tap Search events (the magnifier at the top right) and type: it searches title, venue, city, client and notes.
  2. Narrow with ALL, GIGS, REHEARSALS, QUOTES (admins only) and PAST; tap a result to open it.
  3. Close search (top left) or Escape closes and clears the search.

Upcoming events come first, soonest first, then past ones, most recent first. With no match: No matching events.

Search is the rail’s Search. On an open device it stays on the left page and the event you pick opens on the right.
The event you pick opens beside the list and stays outlined in the results.
Back first hides the keyboard, then closes the search. An event opened from the results closes first, back to the results.

Open a day or add an event from the grid

  • A day with one event opens that event.
  • A day with several events opens the {count} events panel under the grid: pick the event, or Close day.
  • An empty day opens Free day. Admins see Nothing scheduled yet. Add something for the band: and the Gig, Rehearsal and Quote tiles to start an event on that date.
  • Dismiss date selection (the X) or another tap on the same day closes the panel.
The tiles start the gig, rehearsal or quote wizard with the date already filled in.
A free day shows Nothing scheduled on this day. with no tiles; for a date of your own: Create → Add personal gig (personal gigs).
A dashed, faded day is a saved draft: tapping it opens the draft, not a new event (drafts).

Band availability and schedule conflicts

When an admin picks a date in the gig wizard, the picker counts how many active members have nothing else on that day, in any of their calendars. Counts only: never who is busy or why.

The Event date picker

  • Days run from grey to green: the greener, the more members are free. Legend: Free in band calendars · {n} members.
  • With a start and end time set, only that window counts (someone busy in the morning is free for the evening); a window past midnight runs into the next day.
  • Under the calendar: {free} of {total} free · no conflicts when everyone is free, otherwise {free} of {total} free.
  • A day with a band event has a grey dot; if you pick it, the sheet stays open, warns (for example Dec 12 has a gig) and offers the next free day, one tap away.
  • Shortcuts: Today, Tomorrow, Weekend.

What counts as busy

  • Gigs and rehearsals not declined, in any band, and personal gigs. Cancelled events and quotes don’t count.
  • A commitment without an end time counts as three hours from its start.
  • When you edit a gig, its own lineup doesn’t count as busy.
  • Bands with a single member get no counts.

Conflicts the app points out

  • Invitations: the invitation and the RSVP sheet show No schedule conflicts in your calendar or the other event on that date (for example You’re already booked) (answer an invitation).
  • Personal gigs on the date of a band gig or rehearsal: SCHEDULE CONFLICT, with a button to tell that band’s admin (personal gigs).
Personal gigs are private: bands only see that the person is busy, never the title, venue or fee.
In the composer the month sits right in the Date & time card, with the same counts under it.
The Planner shows the month’s Free weekends on the top half (the calendar on iPad and Duo).

Creating and managing a gig

Create a gig

Only admins create gigs; members log their outside dates as personal gigs.

Start

  1. Create (the + in the bottom bar) → New gig; or a free day → Gig, or Gig on an empty calendar.
  2. Step 1 of 3 · Event & lineup opens. The three segments at the top show the step; tap one to go back to a step you have already reached.

Step 1 · Event & lineup

  1. Event title: left empty, the app builds one from the type and the client (for example "Wedding - Client A"), or "Club Gig" without a client.
  2. Client / promoter (optional).
  3. Event type, required: Wedding, Corporate, Private party, Festival, Club, Pub, Other.
  4. Lineup preset (Solo, Duo, Trio, Full band) or tick musicians one by one; Customize opens the roster, Done folds it.
  5. A fee next to each name, or Fee to be discussed (TBD); an Extra band fee / services if needed (lineup and fees). Then Next.

Step 2 · Date & venue

  1. Venue / location: search and pick a result, or a quick chip: TBD, Main Stage, Private Venue.
  2. Event date, the only required field of the step; the picker shows who is free (availability).
  3. Start time and End time (20:00 and 23:30 to start with); the length shows next to End time.
  4. Add under Load-in (optional) and Soundcheck (optional) for call times. Then Next.

Step 3 · Review & confirm

  1. The summary (title, date and times, venue, lineup); Edit lineup and Edit date & venue go back.
  2. Setlist & songs (optional): a saved setlist from Load from templates, songs from the library with Add songs, more sets with Add set (build a setlist).
  3. Logistics, guests & setting (optional): Setting (Indoor/Outdoor), Estimated guests, Performance duration.
  4. Tasks & band notes (optional): a list from Load a to-do list, tasks typed and added with Add, Band notes for directions.
  5. Create gig.

After Create gig

  • The gig is saved as confirmed and appears on the calendar of the whole lineup.
  • If the admin is on the lineup, they are confirmed; everyone else is pending and gets an invitation showing only their own fee (notifications). The admin gets an inbox note: the gig is waiting for the others to confirm.
  • If the save fails, the wizard stays open with what was typed, and the gig is kept in the drafts too.
  • With no connection the gig is saved on the device: Saved offline • Will sync when connected.
Closing the wizard after typing something asks Save this gig as a draft? (drafts).
A new gig opens as a one-screen composer with a live preview instead of the three steps (gigs on iPad).
On step 1 the left page shows the gig as the band will see it, filling in as you type; on steps 2 and 3 the calendar stays on the left.
Close gig wizard sits in the rail, under the clock.
The back gesture closes the open song picker first, then offers to save a draft.

Lineup and fees in the gig wizard

  • Lineup preset picks the first 1, 2 or 3 people of the roster, or everyone with Full band; a preset always includes the admin. Their row, You · {role}, starts ticked: untick it when the admin doesn’t play. They still manage the gig, with no fee, invitation or RSVP of their own.
  • The card reads Roster · {count} of {total}; each musician is ticked one by one, with the instrument from the band roster (or Admin / Member when none is set).
  • Folded, the card shows up to five avatars, the musician count and {amount} est. total.

Each musician’s fee

  • Their default fee from the band roster, when they have one; otherwise the band’s pricing settings: the core-member rate for the first core members, the session-musician rate for the others (payments and income).
  • Any amount can be typed in the Fee for {name} field.

Band money

  • Extra band fee / services: what the band charges on top of the fees; chips such as + Sound (€300) add the active services from the pricing settings.
  • Band fee = musicians’ fees + extra; whatever is not paid out to musicians is the Band fund.
  • Fee to be discussed (TBD): the date is confirmed now, the money later. The fields hide, every fee is saved as 0, invitations say the fee is to be discussed and the gig shows To be discussed.
Each musician only ever sees their own fee, everywhere (invitation, card, gig). Other fees, the extra and the band total stay with admins.
If a musician’s fee is changed later, only that musician is notified.

Gig times, venue and logistics

  • Start time and End time frame the show; an end earlier than the start means it runs past midnight (21:00–02:00 is one gig that ends the next morning).
  • A gig stays upcoming until its day is over or, past midnight, until the show really ends; then it becomes a past gig.
  • Load-in (optional) and Soundcheck (optional) are call times: they appear as LOAD IN and SOUNDCHECK on the gig, on the member’s Home card and in Stage mode. Nothing is invented: a time nobody set reads TBD.
  • Venue / location: pick a suggestion, with one tap even with the keyboard up. A named place fills in the venue and its street; a plain address becomes the venue itself. City, region, postcode and country are filled in too.
  • The search finds venues by name (a club, a hotel, a restaurant) as well as addresses, through Google Maps: picking one fills in its address and its position on the map. Places near the last venue you picked come first. When Google can't be reached, the search uses OpenStreetMap, which finds mostly addresses. The list's header says which one answered: Google Maps or OpenStreetMap.
  • On the gig, the venue name (or MAP) opens the maps app; Logistics & notes has Copy for the full address and Maps.
  • Leave the venue empty or TBD when it isn’t known: the card warns Missing venue.
  • For a gig in the next seven days with a city, the wizard shows Forecast: {temp}° {sky} and the sunset time; the gig’s header shows the weather until the day has passed.
  • Setting: Indoor or Outdoor; editing keeps the saved value (also HYBRID, from quotes) unless you change it.
  • Estimated guests: minus and plus (steps of 10) or the chips 50, 100, 150, 200, 300+. Performance duration: from 15 minutes to 6 hours.
Sets and changes every field.
Sees the times, venue, address, setting, guests, dress code and band notes; is notified when the date, times, call times or venue change.
Sees the date, load-in, soundcheck, show times, venue, city and address of the gig they are asked for.

Create a gig on iPad

On iPad (or another wide window without the dual-screen rail) New gig opens the composer: the same fields, the same gig as the step wizard. Once created, the gig is changed right on its page (edit a gig).

  1. Create → New gig.
  2. 1 Event: type, title, client.
  3. 2 Date & time: a day in the month, Start time and End time; under the month, how many members are free.
  4. 3 Venue: the venue search.
  5. 4 Lineup: a preset or ticked musicians, with their fees to check.
  6. 5 Fee: Fixed or TBD; it shows Musicians {amount} and Band {amount}.
  7. Extras from the buttons under the cards: Setlist, Rider & stage, Logistics (call times, setting, guests, duration), Tasks; each opens a sheet that closes with Done.
  8. Check the Live preview (As the band sees it) and the checklist under it.
  9. Create gig, or Save draft to finish later.

Create gig needs a type and a date. In landscape the cards and the preview sit side by side.

With Rider & stage the stage plot and the rider are prepared before the gig exists, and saved with it when you create it (rider and stage plot).

Save and continue a gig draft

  1. Close the wizard after typing something: the X (Close gig wizard), Cancel or Back.
  2. In Save this gig as a draft?: Save draft saves it, Discard throws it away, Keep editing goes back.
  3. To pick it up: Drafts on Home, its day in the calendar (dashed and faded) or the DRAFTS filter. The sheet shows what it holds and Still a draft: only you can see it.
  4. Continue editing reopens the wizard where you left it; Delete draft and then Delete removes it.
Drafts belong to the admin who made them: members get no notification and don’t see the gig until it is created.
  • Drafts are kept on the device (they work offline) and sync to the admin’s other devices.
  • A draft without a date appears only under Drafts on Home.
  • A failed save becomes a draft automatically; once the gig is created, the draft is removed.
  • Reopening a draft and choosing Delete draft in the leave sheet removes it from Drafts too.
Deleting a draft can’t be undone; nothing had been sent to the band, so nobody else is affected.

Edit a gig

A gig is changed right on its page, one part at a time.

  1. Open the gig. The admin finds the controls on the page itself: the pencil at the top, the pencil next to the venue, the time tiles and EDIT next to a section.
  2. A sheet opens with only that part, for example Title, date & times, Venue & location or Lineup.
  3. Make the changes and tap Save, which stays off until something changed. Cancel, ✕, Back or a swipe down closes the sheet and leaves the gig as it was; if you changed something, Save your changes? asks first: Save changes, Discard or Keep editing. Opening another part of the gig asks the same.
  4. The setlist is edited right in the SETLIST section, with its own Save (edit the setlist).

What each part changes

  • Title, date & times (the pencil at the top, or a time tile): title, event type, date, start and end, load-in and soundcheck.
  • Venue & location (the pencil next to the venue, or the venue when it reads TBD): venue name, address, city and Setting.
  • Lineup (EDIT next to LINEUP, or ADD MUSICIAN): a Lineup preset or who plays, with their fees. The pencil on the fee card (Edit fee): the fees, Fee to be discussed (TBD) and the extra band fee.
  • TASKS and CLIENT & NOTES: EDIT to add, rename or delete to-dos (or Load a to-do list) and to change the client and the Notes for the band.

What the band is told

  • New musicians get an invitation and anyone taken off the lineup is told; everyone already on it keeps their answer.
  • A new date, time, call time or venue: one Gig updated notification listing the changes.
  • A changed fee: notified to that musician only.
  • Setlist: one notification with the songs moved, added or removed.
Past gigs are edited the same way. Members see the page without the pencils and without EDIT.

A quote opens the quote editor instead; a personal gig opens its own form (personal gigs).

Delete a gig

  1. At the bottom of the gig, DELETE (on a past gig: REMOVE FROM ARCHIVE).
  2. Confirm delete, or CANCEL.
  • The gig leaves every calendar; you see Event deleted.
  • Everyone on the lineup who had not declined gets Gig cancelled; deputies who were asked are told too.
  • The gig’s own setlist is deleted with it, unless another event uses it; the songs stay in the library.
  • A quote the gig came from stays in Quotes as a record, without the link to the gig.
Deleting can’t be undone. There is no separate "cancelled" status: deleting is how you call off a gig.
If the gig goes back to negotiation, REVERT TO QUOTE takes it off the musicians’ calendars but keeps their answers (quotes).

Without a connection the delete fails with No connection. Try again when you are online. and the gig stays open.

Rider and stage plot

Every band gig has RIDER & STAGE: OPEN shows the rider, the stage plot, the input list and who brings what.

Set it up (admins)

  1. Technical rider: CREATE RIDER if the gig has none.
  2. Stage plot → EDIT. Under the plot, the first row of chips (Add a musician) adds an instrument: vocals, guitar, bass, keys, drums, percussion, sax or trumpet. The second (Add gear) adds Other musician, a wedge, a speaker or a DI box. Holding an empty spot gives the same choices right there.
  3. Hold an item until it lifts, then drag it and let go; a quick swipe still scrolls the page. A tap on an item changes its short code and caption, or removes it. With a keyboard: Tab to an item, Enter to edit it, arrow keys to move it. Set Layout name, Stage width (m) and Stage depth (m) and tap SAVE PLOT. + NEW LAYOUT and DELETE LAYOUT manage several layouts.
  4. Saving the plot fills the input list for you: each instrument adds its usual channels in desk order (drums: Kick, Snare, OH L and OH R; bass: one DI channel; a DI box: its own line). Removing an item removes its channels.
  5. Next to Input list, EDIT LIST to fine-tune: ADD CHANNEL, change SOURCE, MIC / DI and Who, drag to reorder, SAVE LIST. These changes stay when the plot is saved again. BUILD FROM LINEUP in the editor starts again from the lineup’s instruments.
  6. With no channels yet, BUILD FROM LINEUP under Input list creates the list. If the lineup changes later, the list offers UPDATE FROM LINEUP or KEEP THIS LIST; if the list was edited by hand, BandWith asks first (Rebuild the input list?), because those changes would be replaced.
  7. Next to Who brings what, EDIT: check the list suggested from the lineup, fill in BAND BRINGS and VENUE PROVIDES (one item per line), tick The venue agreed to this once it is settled, SAVE.

Send it to the venue

  • SHARE LINK WITH THE VENUE (or Share rider link) for the link, PDF / Share rider PDF for a file.
  • The rider shows SENT TO THE VENUE {date} and readiness turns to Rider v{version} · sent to the venue. Sharing the PDF counts as sent too; saving it to the phone does not.
  • The venue opens the link in a browser, with no account, and sees the rider, the gig’s times and venue, the stage plot, the input list and who brings what, in its own language when available. Never the client or any fee.
Creates and edits everything, the stage plot included, on every device, and shares the link. The plot of a past gig is read-only.
Everything read-only, plus Share rider PDF. Before anything is set up: The band hasn’t set up the stage for this gig yet.
Rider & stage is a tab of the gig, laid out as a board; in the composer it can be prepared before the gig is created.

Fee and payouts on a gig

  1. The admin taps FEE & PAYOUTS on the fee card (on iPad, the MONEY tab).
  2. The Admin only sheet shows the Band fee, PAYOUTS DUE · {count} with the amount still owed, and MUSICIANS · {paid} OF {total} PAID.
  3. The round {name} paid switch marks a musician paid or not; MARK ALL AS PAID marks every musician who confirmed, has a fee and is not paid yet.
  4. The pencil (Edit fee) changes the fees, Fee to be discussed (TBD) or the extra band fee.
  • Fee card, admins: Band fee, Musician payouts ({count}), Band fund (what stays with the band), Deposit with its share and paid; tags DEPOSIT DUE and To be discussed (Fee TBD).
  • Members: Your fee and PAYOUT DETAILS, which opens Your assigned fee with a paid or not paid badge.
  • After a band gig has taken place, every confirmed musician with a fee above zero and not marked paid is due; pending and declined musicians are owed nothing. Shows that end after midnight count only once they are over.
  • On Home, admins see Payouts due with Review payouts (opens the gig on this sheet); members see Your payout with View payout.
A member only ever sees their own fee and payout; the band fee, other musicians’ fees, the band fund and the deposit are for admins.
Here the band pays its musicians. The client paying the band, invoices and the ledger are in payments and income.

Answers, drop-outs and deputies

Answer a gig invitation

Anyone added to a gig gets a Gig invitation notification and answers from the gig, the inbox or the RSVP sheet.

  • From the gig (header INVITE PENDING): Your RSVP · Are you in? → ACCEPT or DECLINE. If you answered maybe in the past, the card says Your answer was Maybe. Choose GOING or CAN'T.: pick one of the two.
  • From the inbox (the bell → Gig invitation): Invitation details with the date, Timings, venue and Allocated fee (your own fee); Schedule conflict or No schedule conflicts in your calendar; then Accept event or Decline. Add to Google Calendar adds the date to your calendar.
  • From the RSVP sheet Can you make it?: date, time, Timings, venue, Your fee and Lineup so far · {count} of {total} in (who plays, with instrument and answer). Choose GOING or CAN’T MAKE IT, add a Note for the band (optional) if you like, and send with Send RSVP.
  • Accepting: You’re confirmed! and a notice to whoever created the gig.
  • Declining: You dropped out of this event., a notice to every admin of the band that a replacement is needed, and the gig leaves your calendar.
The invitation shows only the recipient’s own fee (or to be discussed); the lineup list shows names, instruments and answers, never fees.

A quote can ask you to keep a date free: on Home, the card Possible gig – keep the date free shows the day, the time and the place, with I'm free and I can't. The answer is about the date only; the gig arrives when the admin confirms it (see Date on hold: who is free before the client says yes).

Drop out of a gig

  1. At the bottom of the gig, DROP OUT.
  2. At Drop out?, Confirm drop (or CANCEL).

You dropped out of this event. appears and the gig leaves the member’s calendar; every active admin of the band and the gig’s creator are notified that a replacement is needed.

You can’t undo a drop-out yourself: to play after all, an admin has to add the member to the lineup again.

What a deputy sees

A deputy is not a band member and never opens the workspace: they see the request, from the link in a message, or in the app under Deputy gigs if their account is linked to the band.

  • The band and the gig title; Date, Load-in, Soundcheck, Show (start and end), Venue, City, Address; their Role and the Fee offered.
  • Open in Maps, Add to calendar, Google Calendar and Subscribe to my gigs (a calendar feed of all their deputy gigs).
  • In Can you make it? they answer Yes, Not sure yet or No, I can’t, with an optional Message to the band. The status becomes You said yes, You’ll confirm later or You said you can’t; Change your answer changes it.
  • If the date, times or venue change afterwards: Something changed with the old and new values, and the question Still OK.
  • Deleted gig: Gig cancelled; withdrawn request: Request withdrawn; finished gig: Gig over.
A deputy sees only their own fee and the logistics: never the client, the band fee, other musicians’ fees or the band’s finances.

What admins, members and deputies see on a gig

Members and deputies see only their own fee and the logistics. The rules are enforced by the server, not only hidden in the app: a member’s phone never receives the band fee, the client or other people’s fees. When your role in a band changes, the phone drops what it kept of that band for offline use and loads it again for the new role: as an admin you see the client, as a member never.

Personal gigs, archive and Home

Log a personal gig

An outside date only the person who logs it can see: it blocks the date for their bands and keeps track of what they earn.

  1. Create → Add personal gig (members) or Log personal gig (admins).
  2. Required: Gig title *, Date *, Start *, End *; optional: Venue / location (optional), My personal fee (optional), Personal notes (optional).
  3. Create personal gig.
Bands only see that the person is busy on that date, in the availability counts; the title, venue, fee and notes stay private.
  • A pink header with PERSONAL and ONLY YOU; the CALL, START and END tiles.
  • If a band gig or rehearsal falls on the same date: SCHEDULE CONFLICT; NOTIFY {band} ADMIN warns that band’s admins and then reads ADMIN NOTIFIED.
  • EARNINGS: My fee and a Paid switch; these fees count in Earned in the archive.
  • EDIT and DELETE (then Confirm delete).

No lineup, setlist, rider or chat. To play it with others, the form offers Create a free band.

A gig becomes past at the end of its day or, if it runs past midnight, when the show ends.

Live updates and working offline

  • When someone changes the lineup, the tasks or the setlist, the open gig reloads that part at once; when someone saves the gig, the open one takes the new values, keeping your own unsaved setlist edits.
  • Payouts marked paid update Home straight away.
  • A gig created or edited offline is saved on the device with Saved offline • Will sync when connected; its card shows Sync until the gig, setlist, tasks, lineup and rider reach the server.
  • Drafts are kept on the device and work offline.
  • The setlist of a gig you opened before is kept on the phone: Stage mode works with no signal.
  • Deleting a gig needs a connection.

FAQ

Why can’t a member create a gig?
Only admins create gigs. A member can log a personal gig or propose a rehearsal from Create.
Why did a gig disappear from the calendar?
The member declined it or dropped out, an admin took them off the lineup, or the gig was deleted. A gig turned back into a quote also leaves members’ calendars.
How do I confirm the date before the fee is agreed?
With Fee to be discussed (TBD): the gig is confirmed and invitations say the fee is to be discussed; set the fees later with the pencil on the fee card (Edit fee).
Can I duplicate a gig?
There is no duplicate feature: create a new gig and load the same setlist from Load from templates and the same tasks from Load a to-do list.
Can band gigs go into the phone’s calendar?
From an invitation, with Add to Google Calendar. Deputies can also use Subscribe to my gigs for a live feed. Every member can subscribe to all their events in Settings › Calendar sync (Sync your events with your calendar).
Can I edit the stage plot on my phone?
Yes, on every device: hold an item to move it, or an empty spot to add one there. The plot of a past gig is read-only.

Repertoire, setlists and Stage mode

All of the band's songs live in one library, the Repertoire. Admins build the gig setlists from it, and on stage everyone reads lyrics and chords while staying on the same song as the rest of the band.

The Repertoire and its songs

The Repertoire screen

The Repertoire is the band's song library. Every song in it belongs to the band, and every active member of the band can read every song. Setlists are built from these songs.

Open the Repertoire

  1. On Home, tap the Repertoire tile under Quick Actions.
  2. Or tap your avatar at the top (Open Identity and Account Hub) and tap Repertoire under Band assets & workflows.

What is on the screen

  • Songs and Setlists at the top switch between the two lists. Each shows how many there are.
  • The search field filters the list you are on: Search songs, artists… matches titles and artists, Search setlists… matches setlist names.
  • The Band repertoire card shows the number of Songs and Setlists, the Total duration of all songs, and Status readiness: one dot per song, coloured by status, with the counts for Ready, Learning and Suggested below.
  • Each song row shows the title, the artist and key, the duration and the BPM. A Suggested or Learning tag shows when the song is not ready yet.
  • The round Play button at the left of a row plays the song's recording or opens its link. It is greyed out when the song has neither.
  • Tap anywhere else on a song row to open its menu (see Song menu: every action).
  • Above the songs, admins see Suggestions from members when something waits for review; members always see My suggestions (see Review members' suggestions and Suggest a change to a song).

Add a song to the repertoire

A song goes in with three short steps; only the title is required.

  1. In the Repertoire, on Songs, tap Add song (the + button at the top right). With an empty library you can also tap Add first song.
  2. On step 1, Song, type the Song title * and, if you know it, the Original artist. To bring in chords or lyrics now, tap Import chords and lyrics (see Import chords and lyrics into a song).
  3. Tap Next. On step 2, Details · optional, fill in Duration, BPM, Key and Genre if you want.
  4. Tap Next. On step 3, Files & links · optional, add a recording, a chart, links, notes or lyrics.
  5. Tap Add song. The button turns into Added! and the form closes.

You can save at any step: tap Add song now under the fields to save with what you have so far.

If a song with the same title and artist is already in the library, the form says “… is already in your library. Save again to add a second copy.” Tapping the save button again creates a second copy.

What happens after you save

  • The song is added with the status Ready.
  • Everyone else in the band gets a note in their notifications inbox (no push).
  • Files you picked are uploaded when you save, into the band's private storage. If the save fails, they are removed again.

Edit, approve or delete a song

Edit a song

  1. In the Repertoire, tap the song row, then tap Edit.
  2. Change what you need on any of the three steps. Tap Save now to save from step 1 or 2, or Next and then Save song on step 3.

Only the fields you changed are saved, so a bandmate's change to another field at the same time is kept. The rest of the band gets one push, for example “Song updated · Paper Boats” with “Leo M. changed: key, tempo.”

Change a song's status

  1. Tap the song row.
  2. Tap Mark as ready, Approve · learning or Back to learning. The tag on the row changes at once.

Delete a song

  1. Tap the song row, then tap Delete.
  2. The confirmation names the setlists that lose the song and the upcoming gigs that play it.
  3. Tap Delete to confirm, or Cancel.
Deleting a song removes it from every setlist that has it, and its uploaded recording and chart are deleted for good. This can't be undone.
Edits, changes the status of and deletes every band song.
Cannot edit or delete band songs. Uses Suggest a change instead (see Suggest a change to a song).

Lyrics, chords and key

How lyrics and chords are written

Lyrics are plain text with a few simple marks. The same text holds the words and the chords, so one song serves the singer and the players.

The marks

  • A section starts with a heading line: # Verse 1 or [Chorus].
  • A blank line separates stanzas.
  • A chord goes in brackets right before the word it falls on: [Am]word. In the reader it shows above that word.
  • **bold** and *italic* work inside a line.
  • JSON also works, for example a list of lines or a list of sections with a name and lines.

What the app tidies when the song is saved

  • Section names are put in one form: “Rit.” becomes Chorus, “[Strofa 2]” becomes Verse 2. Names in Italian, Spanish, French, German and Portuguese are understood.
  • Verses without numbers are numbered when there are several.
  • A line of chords above a line of words (chords-over-lyrics sheets) is merged into inline chords.
  • A chord standing before a space is attached to the next word.

Section names are written in English in the text, but each person sees them in their own app language.

If the lyrics can't be drawn, the reader says “These lyrics can’t be shown. Check their format in Edit song.”

Read lyrics and chords

The full-screen reader lets you switch views, change the text size and transpose for yourself.

  1. In the Repertoire, tap the song row and tap Lyrics or Chords. From the mini player, tap Lyrics.
  2. Switch between Lyrics, Chords and Structure with the tabs under the title. Only the views the song has are shown.
  3. Tap the − and + buttons (Smaller text, Bigger text) at the top to change the text size.
  4. To read in another key, use − and + around the key under the tabs. Tap the key to go back to the original.
  5. Tap X (Close lyrics) to close the reader.
ViewWhat it shows
LyricsThe words only, chords hidden.
ChordsThe words with their chords above them, or the iReal chart if the lyrics have no chords.
StructureThe iReal chart bar by bar, or each section's chord progression in boxes.

Transpose for the whole band

  1. Open the song's menu and tap Edit, then Next to reach Details · optional.
  2. Under Key, tap Transpose down a semitone (−) or Transpose up a semitone (+). The number between them shows how far you moved.
  3. Tap Save now. The band is notified that the key and chords changed.

  1. In the song form, on Details · optional, tap Tap in time with the song. After two taps the BPM fills in; the count of taps shows next to the button.

  3. If the result is half or double the real tempo, tap ÷2 or ×2.

Edit a song's chords

In the chord editor you put chords on words by tapping, move them by dragging and copy them between sections.

  1. In the Repertoire, tap the song row and tap Edit chords. You can also tap Edit chords in the song reader's header.
  2. Tap a word. The bar at the bottom shows Chord on “…” and the chords the song already uses.
  3. Tap a chord to put it on the word, or type a new one in New chord, e.g. F#m7 and tap Add. No chord removes it, Done closes the bar.
  4. To move a chord, drag it onto another word.
  5. To reuse a verse's chords, tap Copy chords from… above a section and pick the section to copy from.
  6. If the song has an iReal chart, tap From iReal (…) above a section to lay the matching chart section's chords over its lines.
  7. Tap Undo at the top right to step back. When you are done, tap Save chords.

Checks the editor makes

  • Tidy the format shows when section names or chords are not in the band's format; one tap fixes them.
  • “Not in the iReal chart: …” lists chords in the lyrics that the chart never plays.
  • “The chords sound in …, the song is in ….” shows when the chords don't fit the song's key, with a button to transpose them.

The chord editor needs lyrics: with none, it says to add them in Edit song first. After you save, the band gets a push that the chords changed.

Leaving with changes not saved asks “Save your changes?” with Save changes, Discard and Keep editing.
Edits the band's chords for everyone.
Has no chord editor. Suggests a chord correction with Suggest a change (see Suggest a change to a song).

Import, listen and annotate

Import chords and lyrics into a song

Chords and lyrics can come from the iReal Pro forum, a file or the clipboard.

  1. In the song form, tap Import chords and lyrics on step 1, or Paste or import lyrics on step 3.
  2. Pick a tab: From the forum, From file or Lyrics.
  3. From file: tap Choose a file and pick a MusicXML (.musicxml, .mxl), ChordPro (.cho, .chordpro, .pro) or text (.txt) file.
  4. Lyrics: copy the lyrics elsewhere, then tap Paste from clipboard, or paste them into the box and tap Preview.
  5. Check the preview and tap Use these lyrics, or Back.
  6. Save the song as usual.
  • Lyrics always show in a preview first. If the song already has lyrics, the preview says these replace them.
  • A chord chart on its own is attached straight away, without a preview.
  • Whatever the import brings fills in empty fields only: a title, artist, key and tempo you already typed stay.
  • When pasting, empty lines separate sections; chords above the words or in [brackets] stay with them.
  • From iReal Pro on your device: Share › Export › MusicXML, save it to Files, then use From file.
Only your band sees the lyrics and charts you import. Import only what you have the right to use.
Imports into any song while adding or editing it.
Imports while suggesting a new song. For an existing song, suggests the change instead.

  2. Tap Browse the iReal Pro forum. The forum opens inside the app.
  3. Tap a song or a playlist link. Its chords come straight back to the form.

  • The chart shows in the song reader's Chords and Structure views, and the chord editor can use it to place chords.

Import a song list

Add many songs at once from a CSV or text file, or load the curated repertoire.

  1. In the Repertoire, on Songs, tap Import song list (the upload button at the top right). With an empty library you can also tap Import list (CSV/TXT).
  2. Pick a .csv, .tsv or .txt file, up to 2 MB.
  3. The line above the list says how many songs were imported and how many were already in your library.

What the file can look like

  • A spreadsheet saved as CSV (Excel, Numbers, Google Sheets) with a header row. Columns that are read: title (or song, name, track), artist (or band, performer), key, bpm (or tempo), duration (or length, time; m:ss or seconds) and genre.
  • A plain list typed in Notes: one song per line, as “Title - Artist”, “Title, Artist” or just “Title”. Numbered lines and blank lines are fine.
  • Up to 2000 songs per file.

Songs with the same title and artist as one already in the library are skipped. Imported songs are added as Ready.

Load the curated repertoire

  1. With an empty library, tap Load 700+ songs.
  2. The app adds the curated songs that aren't in your library yet and says how many.

My notes and files on a song

Anyone can add private notes on a song, a line, a word or a chord, and keep their own files with it.

Your notes and files are yours only: nobody else in the band sees them, admins included. The band's song doesn't change for the others.

Add a note

  1. Open the song in the reader (Lyrics or Chords) or in Stage mode.
  2. Tap Add note (on Stage, Note). My notes turn on by themselves; the first time a sheet explains what they are: tap Got it.
  3. A bar at the bottom of the screen says Tap the line for your note: tap the line you want, or Whole song for a note on the whole song. Cancel stops.
  4. Quicker: hold a line for half a second (on a computer, right-click it). Note on this line opens: tap Note for me.
  5. For a line, choose what the note is on: Whole line, one of its words, or one of its chords.
  6. Type in My note and tap Save note.
  7. Note saved on “…” tells you where it went.
  • Your notes show under their line, highlighted. Notes on the whole song show above the lyrics.
  • Tap a note to change it, or remove it with Delete note.
  • A note stays on its line even when an admin edits the chords or moves sections. If its line is no longer in the song, it moves to the top so it isn't lost.
  • Turn My notes off to hide them, for example on stage. The switch is kept on this device.

Add your own file

  1. With My notes on, tap Add file and pick a recording or a score.
  2. It shows as a chip under the bar. Tap it to open it; tap its X and confirm to remove it.

To share a note with the band, tap Suggest to the band in the note sheet. It goes to the admin as a suggestion, with the line it was on.

In Stage mode the bar shows Note and an icon for Add file, so it stays on one row above the lyrics.

Members' suggestions

Suggest a new song

A member proposes a song for the repertoire; an admin approves or rejects it.

  1. In the Repertoire, on Songs, tap Suggest a song (the + button at the top right).
  2. Fill in the form as for any song: Song title * is the only required field. You can import chords and lyrics and attach a recording or a chart.
  3. Tap Suggest now to send it early, or go through the steps and tap Suggest a song on the last one.

What happens next

  • The song appears in the Repertoire with a Suggested tag, for everyone in the band.
  • The band's admins get a push: “Song suggested · …”. Several songs suggested within a minute send one push.
  • When an admin approves it, it moves to Learning and you get a push. If it's rejected, the song and its files are deleted, and you get a push with the admin's reason if they gave one.

Files you attach to a suggested song are kept in your own folder until an admin accepts the song.

Suggest a change to a song

A member sends the admin a correction to the lyrics or chords, or a note for the whole band.

  1. In the Repertoire, tap the song row and tap Suggest a change.
  2. Choose Lyrics or chords or Note for the band.
  3. For lyrics or chords, change the text in Lyrics with chords. Chords go in brackets before the word: [Am]word. The line under the box says what you changed, for example “Chords changed on 2 lines”.
  4. Add a Message for the admin (optional) if it helps.
  5. For a note, type it in Note for the whole band.
  6. Tap Send suggestion. The sheet says Sent to the admin and closes.
The band's song stays the same for everyone until the admin accepts the suggestion.

You can also start from one of your notes: open it and tap Suggest to the band. The note is sent with the line it was on.

The admins get a push named after the kind: “Lyrics correction · …”, “Chords correction · …” or “Note for the band · …”.

Follow or withdraw your suggestions

  1. In the Repertoire, on Songs, tap My suggestions. Its line says how many wait for the admin.
  2. Waiting lists the ones still open; Reviewed lists the rest with their outcome.
  3. Tap one to see it. Changes, Before and After show the difference; a reviewed one shows the admin's reason if they gave one.
  4. To take a waiting suggestion back, tap Withdraw suggestion, then Withdraw suggestion again to confirm, or Keep it.

Setlists

Build a setlist

A setlist is created in the Repertoire: add songs from the library, put them in order and save.

  1. In the Repertoire, tap Setlists, then New setlist (the + button at the top right). With no setlists yet you can also tap Create first setlist.
  2. Type a Setlist name *, for example Jazz night 1st set, and tap Create setlist.
  3. Tap the new setlist in the list to open it.
  4. Tap Add songs from library…. Search with Search library… or tap a genre chip to filter. Tap a song to add it; tap it again to take it out. Songs in the setlist are highlighted.
  5. Tap Done to close the library.
  6. Drag a song by its grip (Drag to reorder) to move it. Tap X on a row to remove it.
  7. Change the Setlist name at the top if you want.
  8. Tap Save setlist.

What the editor shows

  • Setlist order (… songs) with the Total running time.
  • When the setlist has more than one set, a tag per set with its songs and time, and a Set 1, Set 2 tag on the first song of each set.
  • How many songs have no duration: add one with Edit for an exact total.

After you save, the band's active members get one push with what changed, for example “Setlist updated · Rooftop set” and “Leo M.: 2 songs moved, 1 added.” A rename is mentioned too.

Leaving the editor with changes not saved asks “Save the setlist changes?” with Save changes, Discard and Keep editing.

Setlists, sets and templates

A setlist is an ordered list of band songs, in which each song appears only once. It can be split into sets (Set 1, Set 2…), for example before and after a break.

  • Sets are made when you set up a gig's setlist: Add set in the gig form, or New set when adding songs in the gig overview.
  • In the Repertoire's setlist editor you see the sets and can move songs between them by dragging across the boundary; a new song joins the last set. Empty sets disappear when you save.
  • Setlists marked as templates show a Template tag in the list. The gig form offers your saved setlists under Load from templates.
  • When you load a saved setlist into a gig, the gig gets its own copy, named after the gig (for example “Rooftop Session setlist”). Changing the gig's setlist later doesn't change the one you loaded from.

Stage mode

Stage mode

Stage mode is the black, glanceable live screen with the setlist and each song's lyrics or chords: on gig day with the run of show, at a rehearsal as Live mode.

Open Stage mode

  1. Open an upcoming band gig and tap the Play button at the top right (Open stage mode). On gig day it is highlighted.
  2. Or, on Home, tap Go live under your next gig.
  3. To leave, tap X (Exit stage mode).
  4. On an upcoming band rehearsal the same button is Open live mode. It opens Live mode (below); to leave, Exit live mode.

What is on the screen

  • Stage mode · <date>, the gig title, venue and city.
  • The run of show: Load in, Soundcheck, Show and End in 24-hour time. The next one is highlighted as Next · …; past ones fade.
  • The Setlist with numbers, titles, key and duration. The current song is highlighted. Songs struck off for tonight are crossed out, and the count above the list says how many (· 2 struck off).
  • A second tab named after your song view (Lyrics, Chords or Structure) shows the current song and follows it as it changes.
  • Prev and Next at the bottom, with the click button between them.

In the song tab

  • Switch between Lyrics, Chords and Structure; the choice is shared with the song reader.
  • Change the text size with − and +.
  • Full screen (the four-corners button at the top) shows the song alone (Song only), from here or from the setlist: one small line with its number and title, then the song; no tabs, times or status. At the bottom there are only Show everything, Prev, the click and Next; arrow keys and Page Up/Down also step, for page-turner pedals.
  • Show everything or Back brings the whole live screen back, on the tab you were on. Leaving Stage mode turns Song only off.
  • Transpose for yourself with − key +. Only your screen changes.
  • My notes shows your private notes over the band's version.
  • The run of show hides to give the song more room.
  • Where there is room (a phone on its side, an iPad, a dual-screen device open over both panes), the setlist stays in a column beside the song. Tap a song there to go to it.

At a rehearsal: Live mode

  • Live mode · <date> and the rehearsal's title at the top.
  • The setlist is the rehearsal's focus songs, in their order. With none picked it says “No songs picked for this rehearsal yet.”
  • Lyrics, chords, transpose, text size, the click and My notes work as on a gig.
  • The admin leads and members follow, over the internet and over Bluetooth, as on a gig. Everyone on the rehearsal can open it.
  • No run of show (no load in, soundcheck, show or end times) and no striking songs off for tonight.

The rehearsal timer

  • The admin taps Start rehearsal in Live mode. The clock runs for the whole band: members see it but don't control it.
  • Pause stops the clock (a break doesn't count) and Resume goes on. End asks first: Keep going or End now.
  • At the end everyone sees the Rehearsal summary: the time rehearsed without breaks and the songs played. The admin can still Resume rehearsal.
  • The session is saved: the rehearsal's page shows it under REHEARSAL SESSION, one per date for a recurring rehearsal. Without internet the admin's clock keeps going and is saved when back online.

Rehearsal notes

  • In Live mode tap Mark when something needs work: it keeps the song and the minute, and you go on playing.
  • At the pause (or in the summary) tap To write and Write each mark: pick what to work on (Tempo, Key, Ending, Solo, Review, Nailed it, or Repeat a section when the song has a structure), add a few words, and say Who it’s for.
  • Any note on a song can say Where in the song: a section and, if you want, one of its lines (Line (optional)). The note's headline shows it, for example Tempo · Chorus · “Lanterns in the rain”.
  • In Live mode you can also hold a line of the lyrics and tap Note for the band: the note opens already on that song, section and line.
  • Admins write for the whole band or any member; members for the band or themselves. The whole band reads every note.
  • Whoever a note is for taps Practised after working on it; an admin taps OK when the band has it (the author can too, on a note to members).
  • The rehearsal's page lists the notes under REHEARSAL NOTES, by song. Notes reach the other phones at once; without internet they wait on the phone.

To practise, and the next rehearsal

  • Home shows To practise: the songs with open notes for you or the band, newest rehearsal first, with Practised on the notes for you. A song opens its page.
  • The song's page shows From rehearsals. A Tempo or Key note that names the value (“Tempo: 96”, “Key: Bb”) gets a button: admins Set on the song, members Suggest to the band.
  • The next rehearsal's page shows FROM LAST TIME, the open notes on its songs, with OK for admins. In Live mode a badge on each song opens them.
  • A new rehearsal offers the songs with open notes as focus songs (From open rehearsal notes); a new rehearsal poll picks them in advance.
  • When a rehearsal ends, everyone with songs to practise gets one push listing them, in their language. Push notifications in Settings turns it off.

Good to know

  • Stage mode is offered for upcoming band gigs and, as Live mode, for upcoming band rehearsals; not for quotes, past events or personal gigs.
  • The screen stays awake while Stage mode is open.
  • Full screen (the four-corners button at the top, in the rail on a dual-screen device) hides the app's bars, the side rail and the phone's status bar, and keeps the screen on. With lyrics or charts in the setlist it shows the song alone (Song only); with none, the setlist gets the whole screen and Exit full screen or Back leaves it.
  • With no setlist, it says “No setlist attached to this gig.”
  • In the phone and tablet apps, the setlist with lyrics and charts is kept on the device when you open the gig, so Stage mode works without internet. Signing out removes it.
  • Without internet and no copy on the device, it says “Offline: the setlist couldn’t be loaded.” with Try again.

Lead the band in Stage mode

As admin, move the current song for everyone, change the order live and strike off songs you won't play tonight.

  1. Open Stage mode on the gig.
  2. Check the status next to the setlist: Live means the band follows you (Band follows you).
  3. Tap Next or Prev to move the band to the next or previous song, or tap any song in the list (Go to …).
  4. To change the order, hold a song until it lifts, drag it to its new place and let go. A quick swipe still scrolls the list, and a tap still makes the song current.
  5. To skip a song tonight, tap the round − button at the end of its row (Strike … off tonight). The title is crossed out and Prev / Next jump over it. Tap + (Bring back …) to put it back.

Follow the leader in Stage mode

As a member, let the admin's phone turn your songs, or step through them yourself.

  1. Open Stage mode on the gig. Following is on: the button next to the setlist says Waiting for leader until the admin's phone is seen, then Following leader.
  2. Your current song, lyrics and chords change when the admin moves on.
  3. To look ahead on your own, tap Prev, Next or a song. Following stops and the button says Follow leader.
  4. Tap Follow leader to jump back to the leader's song and follow again.
  • · BT after the label means you are linked to the leader over Bluetooth.
  • Offline means nothing can reach your phone right now.
  • Members cannot move anyone else's song or change the order.

Use the click in Stage mode

The click is a visual metronome around the screen edge, in time across the band's phones.

  1. In Stage mode, the click button sits between Prev and Next. It shows the current song's BPM.
  2. Tap it (Start the click). A glow flashes around the screen edge on every beat, brighter on the first beat of the bar.
  3. Tap it again (Stop the click) to stop.
Who starts itWhat happens

  • The beats in a bar come from the time signature of the song's iReal chart, otherwise 4.
  • A song without a BPM can't start a click (No BPM for this song). Add the BPM in the song form.
  • Moving to another song hides the click for the song before.
  • Phones keep in time to about 20–40 ms: right for a count-in and a tempo reference, not for an in-ear click.
  • The band's click needs the internet: it isn't sent over Bluetooth.

Play the songs' audio from the stage manager device

One admin's laptop or tablet, plugged into the mixer, is the stage manager: the only device that plays the songs' audio. When it plays a song, every screen in Stage mode moves to that song, even out of the setlist order. Members' phones never play any sound.

Get ready before the gig

  1. Each song needs a Recording (an audio file) in the song form. Songs without one still move the band, but nothing plays.
  2. Plug the device into the mixer (cable tips below) and turn on Do Not Disturb, so notifications don't come out of the PA.
  3. Open the gig while online, open Stage mode and tap Stage manager: play audio here. The device downloads the night's audio: a bar shows how far it is, and the ring around each song's ▶ fills up as it comes down.
  4. Wait until Audio ready counts every song (for example 10/10): from then on it plays with no internet. If a song can't be downloaded, the stage manager says so (see below).

On stage

  1. Tap ▶ on a song to play it. Everyone's screen moves to that song and shows Now playing with the time.
  2. The player at the top has Play / Pause; when a song ends, the same button plays the next one. Stop needs a hold, so a tap by mistake can't cut the music.
  3. A song with no audio, or one that can't play here, says so on the player, and Play becomes Play next with the next song that can play. The band plays the song live meanwhile.
  4. For a song that isn't in the setlist, tap + (Play a song not in the setlist) and pick it from the repertoire. It goes in the setlist right after the current song, for everyone.
  5. To hand over, the other admin taps Stage manager: play audio here on their device, then Take over: the first device's audio stops. Turn off ends it on a device.

The cable to the mixer

  • Laptops, iPads and phones with a headphone jack: a minijack (3.5 mm) cable into the mixer's input, for example 3.5 mm to two 6.3 mm jacks or to two RCA plugs.
  • iPads and iPhones without a jack (USB-C or Lightning): Apple's USB-C (or Lightning) to 3.5 mm adapter, then the same cable. To charge at the same time, an adapter with a USB-C charging port (PD).
  • Mixers with a USB input (many digital mixers): one USB-C cable takes the sound straight in. The same goes for a class-compliant USB audio interface.
  • Never Bluetooth: it arrives late and can drop out. The app warns when the sound goes over Bluetooth or out of the device's speaker.
  • Device volume nearly at the top; set the level on the mixer.
If the cable comes out, the sound can stop or move to the device's speaker. The app shows a warning at once: plug the cable back in.
On a laptop in the browser, the browser keeps the audio: use the same browser and don't clear its data before the gig. Google Drive links download too, when the file is shared as “Anyone with the link”.

Stage mode without internet (Bluetooth)

In the iPhone, iPad and Android apps, the admin's phone also sends the current song and the order over Bluetooth. Members' phones nearby pick it up with no pairing and no internet.

Get ready before the gig

  1. Everyone opens the gig once while online. The app keeps the setlist (with lyrics and charts) and the gig's Bluetooth key on the phone.
  2. Turn Bluetooth on and allow BandWith to use it.
  3. On stage, everyone opens Stage mode. The leader shows Bluetooth or Live · BT; members show · BT after their follow label.

When it doesn't connect

  • “Bluetooth is off or not allowed. Turn it on in Settings to stay in sync without internet.”
  • “This phone has an older setlist. Connect to the internet once to follow over Bluetooth.” This shows when songs were added or removed after the phone last loaded the setlist. A new order alone is fine.
Each gig has its own secret key, given only to the band's active members. Only phones with the key find the leader and accept its messages.
No Bluetooth sync and no copy of the setlist on the device: Stage mode on the web needs the internet.

FAQ

Can members edit songs or setlists?
No. Members read every song and setlist. They suggest new songs and changes to lyrics, chords or notes, and an admin reviews them. Setlists are admin-only to edit.
Who sees my notes on a song?
Only you. Not the band, not the admins. To share one, open it and tap Suggest to the band.
I transposed a song in the reader. Did it change for everyone?
No. Transposing in the reader or in Stage mode changes only your screen. To change the key for the band, an admin uses Transpose for the band in the song form.
Why can't I import a song list?
Importing a list goes straight into the band's library, so it is admin-only. Members suggest songs one at a time.
Does changing a gig's setlist change the saved setlist I loaded it from?
No. A gig gets its own copy, named after the gig. The setlist you loaded from stays as it was.
Does the band get a notification for every song I move?
No. Nothing is sent until you save. Then each person gets one push summing up what changed.
Does Stage mode work without internet?
In the phone and tablet apps, yes: open the gig once while online, and the leader's phone keeps the band in sync over Bluetooth. On the web Stage mode needs the internet.
Can I hear the click?
No. The click is visual: a glow around the screen edge on each beat.
Why does my phone stop following the leader?
Tapping Prev, Next or a song yourself stops following. Tap Follow leader to go back to the leader's song.
What happens to a suggested song that the admin rejects?
It is deleted with the files you attached, and you get a push with the admin's reason if they gave one.
Why don't I see the iReal Pro forum inside the app on the web?
The forum can't be shown inside a web page, so it opens in its own window. Copy a link there and tap Paste link, or drag the link onto the box.
